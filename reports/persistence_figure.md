# When is prediction-led exploitation safe?

_Generated by `scripts/persistence_figure.py`. 30 paired seeds per
condition; `predictor` against `whittle`; paired bootstrap on seeds,
Wilcoxon signed-rank for p. One fixed checkpoint throughout, so the
model is a constant and none of the checkpoint confounds touch this._

![persistence](persistence_figure.png)

## 1. Hypothesis

Temporal **persistence** -- how much of the time an emitter is
detectable -- determines whether prediction-led exploitation helps or
hurts, and emitter **density** does not.

This is worth testing because the obvious reading was the other one.
Across the three tiers a sharper predictor sometimes helped and
sometimes destroyed the scheduler, and the tiers differ in five ways at
once -- emitter count, class mix, interferers, pop-ups and the decoy
penalty -- so "as density rises" was an unsupported reading of a
confounded contrast.

## 2. Controlled result: persistence sets the sign

Emitter count fixed at 15 and the live-channel count fixed; only the
class mix moves.

| condition | emitters <0.5 persistence | det. duty | Δ intercept time | 95% CI | p |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 15 emitters, all persistent | 0.0% | 0.1172 | **+18.0%** | [+0.0, +26.2] | 0.023 |
| 15 emitters, half persistent | 46.7% | 0.0631 | **-30.2%** | [-54.3, -12.7] | 0.023 |

The advantage **crosses zero**: +18.0% when every
emitter is persistent, -30.2% when half are not.
Both are significant at the 5% level, in opposite directions, with
density untouched. Positive means the predictor reaches its first
intercept sooner.

The all-persistent CI reaches +0.0% at its lower
bound, so the *size* of the gain there is weakly determined even
though its sign is not. The claim this supports is that the sign
flips, not that the gain is large.

## 3. Amplification: density scales the magnitude

Persistence profile **identical** at 57.8% of emitters below 0.5; only
the emitter count moves.

| condition | emitters | Δ intercept time | 95% CI | p | Δ emitters missed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 15 emitters, mixed | 15 | -297.5% | [-1778.8, -18.8] | 0.0002 | **+97** |
| 30 emitters, mixed | 30 | -1660.2% | [-2402.7, -794.5] | 0.0000 | **+178** |

The sign does not move; the penalty deepens from
-297.5% to -1660.2% and the excess
emitters missed from +97 to +178.
Density is an amplifier of a cost whose sign persistence has already
decided.

## 4. Boundary condition: the all-scanning anchor is saturated

At 100% low-persistence emitters the detectable duty is
0.0013 and **25 of
30 seed pairs are censored** --
at least one policy never intercepts anything, so there is no finite
time to compare.

The comparison is **withheld**: 5 surviving
pairs is below the 10-pair floor, so no interval is
reported rather than an interval computed from the handful of
seeds that happened to finish.

So it is **not** a third point on the trend of section 2, and is
plotted separately. Folding a degenerate ceiling into a trend line
would disguise an observation-budget limit as a continuation of the
effect -- and would invert its apparent sign, since the surviving
pairs are exactly the seeds where the predictor got lucky.

## 5. Implication for the scheduler

An online scheduler should estimate the **distribution** of temporal
persistence before trusting prediction-led exploitation, not its
average.

Pooled persistence is actively misleading for a mixture: at half
persistent emitters it reads **0.99**, because the always-on emitters
contribute nearly every detectable cell while the scanners hiding
behind them are precisely the ones being missed. An estimator that
tracks the mean would report a regime where exploitation is safe at the
exact point where it costs 88 emitters.

That is the specification for the regime estimator: the quantity to
track is the fraction of emitters below a persistence threshold, which
is a property of the distribution's lower tail and invisible in its
mean.
