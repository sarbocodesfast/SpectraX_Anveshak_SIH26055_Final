## The constraint is not a general improvement

Its value tracks how concentrated the ungated policy already was.

- **`predictor`** parks on channels it is confident about and starves its own
  future reward. Forcing it back across the band trades **11.7% of intercept
  time for 77.3% more interception rate** -- a Pareto trade on the two
  objectives the problem statement names, not a domination.
- **`epsilon_greedy`** parks far worse: its ungated median time to first
  intercept is **1.97 s**. A tight deadline recovers **92.4%** of that and
  **46 emitters**, paying 31.6% of rate.
- **`whittle`** already balances, so the gate buys 15.7% of rate for 34.7% of
  time.
- **`phase_locked`** gives up **41.7% of intercept time for 3.8% of rate**.
- **`phased`** is worse on *all three* metrics at the loosest deadline.

## Why `phased` being harmed is the informative row

`phased` already hands over from sweeping to exploiting when discovery stalls.
A gate on top is redundant -- it enforces something the policy is doing
already -- and the only thing it adds is retunes. Phasing and gating are two
mechanisms for the same job and they are **substitutes, not complements**, so
an adaptive exploration controller should be built on one of them rather than
stacked on both.

## The deadline has a sweet spot, and it moves with the policy

`predictor` peaks at 1200 and is over-constrained at 300, where its rate falls
back to ungated while intercept time keeps degrading. `whittle` prefers 300,
where it reaches its best coverage without losing the rate gain. A single
global constant is therefore wrong in two ways at once: across environments,
and across policies. That is the argument for replacing the fixed deadline
with an exploration debt the controller estimates, rather than for tuning the
constant harder.
