# Does the better predictor fit the dwell budget?

_Generated by `scripts/arch_latency.py`. Forward pass only, batch 1, 100 timed calls on **cpu+cuda**._

Budget: `(t_settle + 1) x dt` = **3.00 ms** on medium. The whole decision must fit, and the forward pass is only part of it -- belief update and argmax are on top.

| device | architecture | params | median | p95 | p99 | forward alone vs budget |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| cpu | `transformer` | 323,905 | 1.033 ms | 1.280 ms | 1.381 ms | 46% |
| cpu | `gru` | 232,705 | 39.402 ms | 54.675 ms | 58.475 ms | 1949%  **over budget** |
| cpu | `tcn` | 95,553 | 7.634 ms | 8.563 ms | 8.807 ms | 294%  **over budget** |
| cuda | `transformer` | 323,905 | 0.678 ms | 0.769 ms | 0.838 ms | 28% |

| cuda | `tcn` | 95,553 | 0.734 ms | 0.796 ms | 0.849 ms | 28% |

## Reading

The GRU's p99 forward pass is **42.3x** the transformer's, on 72% of the parameters. Parameter count is not what the dwell budget constrains: a GRU steps through the window sequentially and a transformer does not.

**This is a deployment constraint, not a detail.** The shipped predictor already sits at 1.5x headroom on MEDIUM (p99 2.031 ms against 3.0 ms) *with the transformer*. Multiplying the forward pass by this factor does not fit, so the ~9% average-precision advantage cannot be taken without either a faster inference path or a larger budget.

Measured on this machine's device; a receiver's target hardware will differ. What transfers is the *ratio* between architectures, not the absolute milliseconds.
